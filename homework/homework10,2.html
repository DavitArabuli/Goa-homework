NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration)

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) is an independent agency of the United States federal government responsible for the country's civilian space program, aeronautics research and space exploration.
NASA was established on July 29, 1958, and officially began operations on October 1, 1958. Its creation marked an important development in the history of space exploration during the Space Race between the United States and the Soviet Union.

History

NASA was created following the Soviet Union's launch of Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite to orbit Earth, in 1957. The United States established the agency to advance its scientific and technological capabilities in space.
One of NASA's most significant achievements was the Apollo program, which successfully sent astronauts to the Moon. On July 20, 1969, Apollo 11 astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to walk on the lunar surface.

Major Missions

NASA has conducted numerous important space missions, including:
Apollo Program: A series of missions that successfully sent humans to the Moon.
Voyager Program: Two spacecraft launched in 1977 to explore the outer planets and interstellar space.
Hubble Space Telescope: A space telescope launched in 1990 that has provided detailed observations of the universe.
Mars Exploration Program: A series of missions designed to study the surface, atmosphere and geology of Mars.
James Webb Space Telescope: A space observatory launched in 2021 to study the early universe, galaxies, stars and exoplanets.
