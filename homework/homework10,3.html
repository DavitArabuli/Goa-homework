Black Hole

A black hole is an astronomical object with an extremely strong gravitational field. Its gravity is so powerful that nothing, including light, can escape once it crosses a boundary known as the event horizon. Black holes are formed when a sufficiently massive amount of matter collapses into an extremely small region of space. They are among the most extreme objects predicted by modern physics.

Formation

Most stellar-mass black holes are believed to form when massive stars reach the end of their lives. After exhausting their nuclear fuel, these stars can collapse under their own gravity.
The resulting object has an enormous density and gravitational influence. Depending on its mass, a black hole can have an event horizon ranging from a few kilometres to millions or billions of kilometres in diameter.

Types of Black Holes

Scientists generally classify black holes into several categories:
Stellar-mass black holes: Formed through the gravitational collapse of massive stars.
Intermediate-mass black holes: Black holes with masses between stellar-mass and supermassive black holes.
Supermassive black holes: Extremely massive objects commonly found at the centres of galaxies.

Event Horizon

The event horizon is the boundary surrounding a black hole beyond which escape is impossible. It is not a physical surface but a limit in spacetime.
Once an object crosses this boundary, it cannot return to the outside universe according to classical general relativity.

Black Holes in Interstellar

One of the most important scientific elements in Interstellar is Gargantua, a fictional supermassive rotating black hole.
The astronauts travel close to Gargantua during their mission to explore potentially habitable planets. Its immense gravitational field causes significant time dilation, meaning that time passes at different rates depending on the observer's location.
The visual appearance of Gargantua was developed with the assistance of theoretical physicist Kip Thorne. The filmmakers used scientific calculations to create a detailed representation of how light bends around a rotating black hole.
